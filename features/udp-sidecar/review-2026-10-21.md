# Review, 2026-10-21: keep the sidecar, fix the page's laws

The owner asked for a review of the sidecar after two weeks of stutter they
could not remove, and whether to change approach or stack. Four reported
symptoms:

- on foot, a small correction at the end of a pan;
- a fine shake of the view while walking;
- no hits on planes, and none on empty tanks;
- hulls in the wrong paint.

## Verdict

Keep the stack. The transport and decoding work, and the server they talk to
is deterministic to the bit. Every defect below is on the page, and none of
them would go away with a rewrite in Rust, Go or WASM. What has to change is
how the predictor's laws are checked. Each law the page predicts with is
measured against the live server, tick by tick, with a node probe. Reading the
decompile and waiting for the next playtest is not enough. Section 2 did this
for the on-foot look law in under a minute of lab time, and found the pan
correction.

## 1. What the lab measured today

These are two node-only probes (`lab/look_law_probe.mjs`) against the lab
(vanilla Wake, co-op, US assault kit). Each sends known words at an even 30 Hz.

| | measured |
|---|---|
| Server tick rate | 30.00/s, from the ghost block's u32 tick against the sidecar clock (four recordings) |
| Ghost rate | 20/s: blocks alternate 1 and 2 ticks |
| Words consumed | one a tick, exactly: delta tick equals delta acked serial on all 416 blocks of a steady run, so no idle ticks and no trims |
| Word to ack, node sender | median 53-71 ms, p90 71-88 ms (about 1.5-2 ticks plus the ghost period) |
| Word to ack, browser page | 4-5 ticks, 133-167 ms (wire.md section 35) |
| Server view law | reproduced to 0.002 deg over 662 acks by the literal law in section 2 |

So the server holds no hidden slack. A word sent evenly is run on the next
tick, and its result comes back in one ghost period.

## 2. The pan correction: the page's look law is a simplification (measured)

`world-soldier-tick.js:33-42` turns the view `3 x axis` a tick standing and
`6 x axis` when the tick's own word moves. That is the average of the engine's
law, but it is not the law. `BFSoldier::handlePlayerInput` keeps a twist
register and a body yaw (the comment above `SOLDIER_MOVING_TURN`,
`viewer/mouse-input.js:687`). Measured, the law is:

```
twist -= d, clamped to [-14, +20]            (this kit; see open questions)
if the twist is inside the clamp (bounds inclusive) and the word does not move:
    d = 0                                    (the torso takes the turn)
body += 3 d
if the word moves and twist != 0:  body -= 3 twist; twist = 0     (the fold)
view = body - 3 twist
```

`lab/look_law_check.py` replays the probe's words through both laws:

| run | acks | literal law vs server | page law vs server |
|---|---|---|---|
| standing and walking pans | 418 | 0.002 deg | 4.5 deg, in two steps |
| presses and releases mid-pan | 244 | 0.002 deg | 12.0 deg, in four 3 deg steps |
| combined plan, from the repo copy | 408 | 0.002 deg | 13.5 deg, in four steps |

The page's law goes wrong on exactly two kinds of tick, and both are common:

- **The tick the twist reaches its clamp mid-step.** A standing pan longer than
  about 42 deg one way (14 units of axis times 3) crosses it. On that tick the
  body turns a whole `3 d` and the twist also moves part of `d`, so the server's
  view turns further than `3 d`, by up to `3 d`. That is 1.5 to 4.5 deg on a
  normal pan. Its correction lands one round trip later, which is the end of
  a short pan or flick.
- **A W, A, S or D press while the twist sits on its clamp.** The clamp has eaten
  this tick's twist, so the fold adds nothing and the server turns `3 d` where
  the page turns `6 d`. That is 3 deg per press at a moderate pan. Strafing
  while aiming does this on every tap.

`createReconciler` then closes each step (`yawShare 0.5` closes it over four
acks), so the view visibly settles about 150-300 ms after the hand stops.
`yawShare` only hides the snap. The fix is the law.

**Fix.** Port the five lines above into the soldier as state, a `twist`
register beside `body.yaw`, and keep `view = body - 3 twist` as the derived
yaw. Then `composedViewYaw` and the fold tracking in `net-sidecar.js:1055`
are no longer needed, because the page holds the same two registers the server
sends. Correct `body` and `twist` from the ack whole, and the residual is
zero. The clamp bounds come from the soldier template and the stance; read
them rather than hard-code -14/+20 (see open questions). Check with
`look_law_probe.mjs` plus a third column in `look_law_check.py` that imports
the page's new law.

## 3. Latency the sidecar adds: the input queue never drains (modelled)

`InputSender` (`sidecar/input.mjs:331-360`, `_tick` at `:452`) releases at most
one page word per 30 Hz slot. A word goes out at once only when the queue is
empty. The page also makes exactly 30 words a second. So any burst becomes a
standing queue that nothing drains: a slow frame whose catch-up ticks send
several words together, a GC pause, a tab switch. The cap is 24 words, which
is 800 ms.

A simulation of that rule, with a 60 fps page and a hitch every 3 to 30 s,
settles at 2.7 words queued (about 90 ms) after the first hitch and never
recovers. That is the gap between the node path (1.5-2 ticks to ack) and the
browser path (4-5 ticks, wire.md section 35). Those numbers fit each other,
but nobody has counted the queue on a live page yet.

What it costs:

- every ack lands about 3 ticks later, so every correction is a longer replay
  and arrives later after the hand stops;
- every shot leaves about 90 ms late, so a plane at 60 m/s needs about 5 m more
  lead than retail does (section 4).

**Fix.** Do not hold a standing queue. Retail's own bound is the server's
buffer of four, with the oldest trimmed (D-2). The sidecar can do as well
without losing words. When the queue is above one, merge the oldest two words
into one: sum the look axes, OR the one-shot presses, keep the newer held
keys. The server then gets one word fewer for that tick. The page's ledger
must map both page seqs to the merged serial, so the ack still lands on the
right tick. Alternatively, have the page run its clock about 2% slow while
`sent - acked` is above target. Count `pending.length` and `sent - acked`
live first, so the fix is measured.

## 4. No hits on planes: drawn about 0.25 s behind the tick that resolves the shot

Evidence: `run.ndjson` (2026-10-21 21:37). In a Chi-ha's MG seat the owner fired
184 rounds at two Zeros 80-260 m away flying 30-64 m/s, and got no `hitMark`.
In the same window a server bot on a Browning took Zero 1475 from 100 to 35 hp
and Zero 1407 from 100 to 53. The hit bool works: the owner's Chi-ha cannon
raised three marks, and earlier recordings have 12 on an SBD and 9 on
Spitfires. The rounds go behind the plane.

The offset adds up to about 0.2-0.27 s:

- the 100 ms remote lookback (`net-sidecar.js:110`, retail's interpolationTime,
  P-2);
- 0-25 ms of packet age;
- the input queue of section 3.

At 55 m/s that is 11-15 m, more than a Zero's half span. The page's own tracers
fly at the drawn plane, so leading until they converge corrects only for the
round's flight time.

Retail has the lookback and the packet age too (no lag compensation anywhere
in the ledger), so retail players lead as well. The part that is ours is the
queue. **Fix section 3 first and count hits again.** Drawing remote aircraft at
the server's present (`sideTime` plus a measured lead, `xfAt` already
extrapolates) would depart from retail. Keep it as a flag, only if parity is
not enough.

Flak never raises the marks on a near miss, because splash damage goes through
`_giveDamage` (XHIT-4). Retail behaves the same.

## 5. No damage to empty tanks: hulls the server scoped out snap back to their spawn (code read)

When the server takes an object out of this client's scope (ghost action 3,
retail's `disableObject`, wire.md section 9), the sidecar writes `k:"d"`
(`sidecar/ghost-writer.mjs:136-142`). The page deletes the pose but keeps the
object (`net-sidecar.js:332-336`). `server-entities.js:407` then places the
entity at `live ?? spawnPos`. So an unmanned hull that was driven off and fell
out of scope is drawn, collidable and shootable at the point where it was
created. The server's hull is elsewhere, or out of this client's view, and no
damage ever comes back. That is the owner's "the tank isn't really there".

**Fix.** Make `k:"d"` a disable: mark the object out of scope, hide its
entity, drop its collider owner and unregister its damageable, and undo that
on the next sample or `k:"o"`. Use `spawnPos` only for an object that has never
had a live pose.

## 6. Wrong paint on hulls: server entities load the base model, not the level's variant (code read)

`netcode-render.js:135-150` builds `${modelsBase}/${modelFileStem(name)}.glb`.
The page's own pad vehicles go through `vehicle-wrecks.js:301-323`
(`variantUrls`), and so do replays (`replay-assets.js:195-215`). Both pick the
level's repainted variant, such as `Sherman.Kasserine_Pass.glb`; 343 of them
exist. A sidecar page draws every hull in the default paint. **Fix:** resolve
`model(tmpl)` through `modelUrls(template)`.

If "the hull colour" meant the minimap instead: retail draws a hull mark grey
until a teammate is inside (MMAP-3, client 0x46b66b), and the page follows that
rule (`map-friendlies.js:216-237`). A wrong colour there would be the crew
lookup. Not settled; one screenshot would say which.

## 7. The driven hull and the walking shake (from netcode-audit.md, agreed)

- The fallback in `correctDrivenHull` (`net-room.js:1189`, audit section 3): an
  ack row without `pos`, `rot` and `vecA` is corrected against the newest pose
  instead of the acked one. That gives 0.3-0.8 m steps at speed. Skip the
  correction instead, and carry the hull state forward in the sidecar.
- The page's hull and soldier integrators are not the engine's, and the
  correction is whole at the ghost rate. On foot, wire.md section 20 measured
  1.8 cm median and 12 cm p90 at the acked tick, which a camera on the body
  shows as a shake. Part of that is section 2: a 3 deg heading error at
  6 m/s splays the paths about 0.3 m/s apart. The rest is the movement law,
  which nobody has measured tick by tick. The probe does it next: add
  position and velocity to the checker and run the page's `Soldier.step`
  under node on the same words.

## 8. Approach

**What to keep.** The Node sidecar: the datagram layer, join, events and ghost
decoding are complete and checked. The page as renderer and predictor. The
retail correction law: take the ack's state whole and replay the pending ticks.

**What to change.**

1. **Measure every predicted law against the server before porting it.** Use
   a node probe that sends scripted words and a checker that replays them
   through the page's module. The server is deterministic and its tick, serial
   and state are on the wire, so a law is either exact or the checker names
   the tick where it is not. The soldier's look is done here; movement, stance
   changes, jump and the land drivetrains come next, aircraft last. This is
   the general fault the owner keeps asking for, as opposed to fixing one
   mechanic at a time.
2. **Until a law is exact, smooth the presentation, not the state.** Take the
   correction whole into the sim, as retail does. Give the camera and the drawn
   body a visual offset equal to the jump, decaying over about 100 ms. This
   differs from `yawShare` or a hull share: those leave the sim off the
   server's curve, while an offset leaves the sim exact and makes only the
   camera's path continuous. Retail needs no offset because its residual is
   zero. Ours stops needing one, object class by class, as section 8.1 makes
   each law exact. It matters most for aircraft, where an exact flight model
   is a long port.
3. **Remove the latency the sidecar adds** (section 3).

**A rewrite on another stack** would carry over every defect above, because none
of them is in the transport. The only rewrite that would change the outcome is
running the engine's own simulation in the browser (the lnxded physics in an
x86 emulator, or decompiled functions compiled to WASM). That is months of
work, and its x87 precision would still need checking. Section 2 shows the
laws can be ported exactly, at least for the soldier, so it is not worth it.

## 2026-10-22: what landed, and what the lab measured

All of it measured against the lab (vanilla Wake, co-op) with a headless page
through its own probe sidecar. The camera is sampled once per drawn frame on
the page's own clock (the sum of the `dt` the page hands `world.step`). The
wobble is each frame's distance from a straight-line fit over +-100 ms.

| | wobble without the blend (`&blend=0`) | wobble with it |
|---|---|---|
| on foot, a straight run | camera direction 0.000 deg, position median 0.1-1.4 mm (varies with the ground the run crossed) | the same |
| M3A1, driven with throttle and steering | position median 2.8 / p90 15.3 mm, direction median 0.016 deg; one 5.9 m / 12 deg lurch | 0.9 / 7.9 mm, 0.005 deg; worst 59 mm |
| SBD, taxiing hard with rudder | position median 11.7 / p90 171 / max 1042 mm | 3.0 / 14 / 32 mm |

- **The torso twist (section 2) is ported.** `soldierYawStep`
  (`viewer/mouse-input.js`) matches the server's view to 0.001 deg on kits 0-4
  standing and kit 0 crouched (`lab/look_law_check.py --viewer`). The clamp is
  the soldier template's `setTurnLeftRightAngle 20 14` with nothing scaling it
  on foot; the seat's left and right angle multipliers (default 0.6) apply only
  when seated. The acked-tick correction replays the unacknowledged ticks from
  the server's twist, and the 0.5 facing share is gone.
- **The blend** (`viewer/correction-blend.js`; section 8.2) eases every
  correction of the soldier and the driven hull into the drawn pose over 0.1 s.
  The simulation keeps the correction whole.
- **Phantom hulls (section 5)** were wider than the scope-out case. Retail
  creates every object from CreateObject disabled (BF1942.exe `0x00493216`,
  `0x0049322c`) and enables it on its first ghost record
  (`GhostManager::readData` `0x08142142`). The page drew every hull it had heard
  of at its creation pose. It now draws, collides with and damages a hull only
  while the server has it in this client's scope.
- **The hull fallback (section 7)** is gone. The sidecar now carries each
  hull's position, rotation and velocity forward per field, and every ack of
  the drives above carried a full state.
- **The queue (section 3)** is reported in each ack (`iq`). The page runs its
  world clock 3% slow while two or more words stand in it. The live depth is
  still to be counted on the owner's page (`__net().inputQueue`).
- **Level variants (section 6)** load for server hulls. On Wake the server
  Sherman was drawn in its paint, star and all. Retail draws an empty hull's
  minimap mark grey (MMAP-3), and so does the page.

**The soldier's ground law is the engine's to 1 mm.** A replay of
`lab/move_law_probe.mjs`'s words matches the server to about 1 mm per ack.
The page differs from it in five places:

- the speed ramp is `sign(r) x r^2`, not linear (PHY-6 as written is wrong);
- the 0.75 x vCmd push applies every tick;
- the soldier is a single-step PhysicsNode with box drag, not a PointPhysicsNode;
- the ground arm is the engine's friction against vCmd, with the `Ny^5`
  budgets and the latch;
- terrain contact pushes out along the checkerboard triangle's normal.

~~The port is in progress, and its acceptance is the page's own code within
2 mm of the server per ack.~~ Landed the same evening (`b4e53153`, `5e5f22eb`).

- The page's whole `Soldier.step` path replays the probe to 0.5-0.7 mm median,
  under 1.3 mm worst, per ack.
- The body ticks at the engine's 30 Hz. At 60 Hz it missed by 3-8 mm.
- The drag areas, 1.14 / 0.50 / 1.14 m^2, come from retail air data:
  lab-server canopies, bail-outs and 98 human free falls. On the ground they
  are indistinguishable from the first fit.
- No vertical speed cap exists in the binary. The recorded "-31.9 m/s" was a
  stuck-contact glitch.

**Vehicles on the ground are the next law.** The SBD's corrections in the
blend-off taxi reached 4.6 m horizontally and 324 mm vertically at p90. The
page's wheels, springs and friction are not the engine's. The engine moves a
vehicle through the same contact and friction solver as the soldier, with the
vehicle grip arms and PhysicsSpring, so the same probe-and-replay method
applies.

## 2026-10-23: section 4 revisited, the lookback runs off the client's clock

Section 4 took retail's 100 ms lookback as measured from the newest sample,
and so called drawing planes at the server's present a departure. It is not:
retail draws at its own game time less 100 ms, and that time is the newest
block's tick plus the actions in flight (ledger P-6, BF1942.exe `0x00491f00`,
`0x00491c90`). The page drew a whole round trip further back than retail.
Fixed in `viewer/net-sidecar.js` (`clientClockLead`); measured in
[wire.md](wire.md) section 38: a bot's plane drawn 0.8-0.9 m from the server's
present, where it was 4-5.6 m.
